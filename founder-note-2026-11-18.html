The Question Before the Search

WILDWIRE Editorial — The Founder's Desk

Start typing a question into a search box and the unfinished sentence may quickly acquire company. A few suggested endings appear beneath it, each offering a route toward something already easy to name. The cursor waits while the question begins to look more settled than it felt a moment ago.

That help can be useful. Suggestions save effort when the goal is finding a familiar place, checking a spelling, or remembering the exact name of something. But they also give attention a path of least resistance: the question that is easiest to complete can crowd out the one that is still taking shape.

Some curiosities arrive in rough form. A book, a passing remark, or a small confusion can leave you with an idea that does not yet fit a neat phrase. Choosing the nearest suggested wording may answer a narrower question than the one that started the search. It can even make the first thought seem silly simply because it is harder to phrase.

There is no need to distrust the suggestions; they are a convenience, not a verdict. Sometimes it is worth finishing the query in your own words, even if the wording is clumsy. The search may take an extra moment, but the question will still belong to you.
