The One-Trip Olympics

WILDWIRE Editorial — The Founder's Desk

A grocery run ends with a familiar engineering problem: how many bags can be carried at once? The answer is usually more than the handles were designed for. One bag hooks over a wrist, another is balanced against a hip, and a carton of eggs is entrusted to whatever patch of space still seems vaguely level.

The second trip would take a minute. Instead, people attempt a complicated shuffle from car to kitchen, negotiating doorways with the focus of a harbor pilot. Keys become inaccessible. A bag threatens to split. The carefully chosen route suddenly includes a staircase. At some point, carrying everything at once stops being convenient and becomes a small performance for an audience of absolutely nobody.

There is something appealing about this very ordinary stubbornness. It is not a major declaration of independence; it is a private refusal to let the groceries win. Still, the extra trip is not a defeat. It is just the option that lets the bread arrive without being compressed beneath a can of tomatoes. The medals, unfortunately, are also imaginary.
