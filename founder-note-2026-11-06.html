The Note Behind the Counter

WILDWIRE Editorial — The Founder's Desk

A growing business can develop a quiet bottleneck when every small decision has to pass through the same person. A package waits because one person knows the unusual packing detail; a customer update stalls because the context lives in someone’s head. Nothing looks dramatic. The day simply accumulates little queues.

Writing down routine choices can seem like stripping work of its craft. But a useful note is not a substitute for judgment; it gives judgment somewhere to begin. A colleague can handle the familiar case, spot what is unusual, and know when to ask. The point is not to make every interaction identical. It is to keep ordinary work from depending on one person’s uninterrupted attention.

There is a particular kind of ambition in making good work transferable. It lets a business serve people consistently without requiring its owner to be everywhere at once. That may leave more room for the decisions that genuinely need their attention—and make the business less fragile when a day goes sideways. Sometimes the infrastructure behind growth is a clear note, kept where the next person can find it.
