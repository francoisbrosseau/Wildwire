The Streetlamp in Two Places

WILDWIRE Editorial — The Founder's Desk

A puddle can make a streetlamp look as if it has slipped loose from its post. Walk a few steps and the image breaks apart; stand still and it gathers again. Neither view is the whole street, though both are real descriptions of what is visible from there.

We often treat disagreement as a contest between a correct picture and a mistaken one. Sometimes that is true. But sometimes people are standing in different weather, carrying different histories, or simply looking from opposite ends of the table. Their accounts may not fit neatly together because the scene itself is larger than either frame.

That does not mean every interpretation deserves equal weight. A reflection can show where the lamp is, but it cannot tell you whether the wiring works. Perspective is useful, not magical: it reveals some details and hides others. The sensible response is to ask what each view can actually establish, and what remains beyond it.

Uncertainty can be less like a fog that must be driven out and more like knowing where the edges of the picture are. Sometimes the next step is not to pick a side, but to move a little and look again. The lamp stays on its post; the puddle gets to keep its version until the next breeze.
