A Table With Several Jobs

WILDWIRE Editorial — The Founder's Desk

By dinner, the kitchen table may have hosted breakfast, a parcel, and a page awaiting a signature, without moving an inch. The furniture stays put; its job description keeps changing.

That shifting purpose can look like disorder if every room is expected to hold one neat identity. But the table is not failing as a dining surface because it briefly became a place to sort the mail. It is being useful in the ordinary, unphotogenic way that homes often are.

Of course, a surface covered in things can be irritating when there’s nowhere to put a plate. A little clearing may be necessary. Still, there is a difference between a room that never gets messy and one that can make space again when the next need arrives.

A chair can be pulled aside for a laundry basket; a corner can become a temporary office, then return to being a corner. The room hasn’t lost its purpose. It has made room for several, and the dishes are already arriving with the next assignment.
