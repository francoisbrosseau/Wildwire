Keep One Thing Off the Scoreboard

WILDWIRE Editorial — The Founder's Desk

There is a peculiar habit of asking every enjoyable thing to justify its existence. A walk should hit a step target. A book should teach something useful. Dinner should look good enough to photograph. Even leisure, apparently, needs a quarterly report.

Measuring things can help. A budget makes spending clearer; a training log can show progress that is easy to miss. The trouble begins when the measurement quietly becomes the reason for doing the thing. A pleasant afternoon starts looking suspicious because it produced nothing that fits into a dashboard.

Try leaving one small corner of life unmeasured. Listen to an album without deciding whether it improves your focus. Cook something because it smells good. Read a few pages without calculating how many books that pace will deliver by December. There is no need to turn this into a fresh challenge with its own streak.

Some experiences earn their place by being enjoyable, absorbing, or shared with someone whose company matters. That can be enough of an explanation. What would you still choose to do if nobody, including an app, was keeping score?
