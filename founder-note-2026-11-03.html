Let the Answer Arrive

WILDWIRE Editorial — The Founder's Desk

Some answers need a little room to arrive. At a dinner table, a question can pass across the plates and meet a pause—not because nobody cares, but because someone is sorting through what they actually think. Meanwhile, forks scrape, water gets poured, and the moment hangs there without dramatic music.

Conversation often rewards quickness. A polished reply keeps things moving; a joke can rescue a lull. But when every pause is treated as a problem, people learn to offer the easiest version of what they mean. The slower answer may be less tidy and more revealing. It can also change the question for everyone else.

Making room for that delay does not require solemn listening or a rule against interruptions. It may be as simple as not supplying the other person’s conclusion, or letting the table move on for a moment before asking again. The point is not to turn every meal into a seminar. It is to remember that attention can sound like quiet. Some conversations get closer not through a perfect response, but through the patience to let one take shape.
